_Review by OpenAI Codex, 2026-09-24, of the flat-widening control's design before it was built. Historical: what was adopted is in [README.md](README.md). Absolute links below point into a review worktree that may no longer exist._

**(a) is a reasonable practical control, but beating it would establish superiority over that widening schedule—not isolate the reset mechanism.** Keep it, with the qualifications below.

1. **Restarts are acceptable; the “≤2×” execution claim is not guaranteed.** The geometric bound applies if cost scales linearly with width. Here, primitive costs, deduplication, frontier exhaustion and early stopping vary with trajectories; an unfinished final restart also spends budget. Charge every restart and report widths attempted/completed, depth reached, termination reason and budget utilization. Starting at 64 preserves its successes whenever the budget covers its first-hit cost. Unspent budget after success is fine; unspent budget because of the cap limits the comparison. Specify whether 16,384 caps width, retained states, visited states or state size. “Widen without restart” cannot recover discarded prefixes without retaining or regenerating them. An anytime/depth-first variant is another algorithm, not inherently a fairer control; I would not build it yet.

2. **Depth is the biggest uncontrolled difference.** Segment-reset permits up to 12 × segment-depth tokens—potentially 576 versus flat’s 48. No width increase makes a >48-token solution reachable by flat. Keep the registered flat-48 control, but add an execution-budgeted flat control with the same total token horizon, or constrain both to 48 for a reset ablation. Per-root width allocation, boundary merging, deduplication and cross-length ranking also change. Beating both flat columns supports the *segment-search package*; attributing the gain specifically to resetting requires tighter ablations.

3. **Executions answer the registered question; wall-clock answers practical efficiency.** Retain execution matching and also report end-to-end solve time, on the same host/device with controlled timing. Your [metrics convention](/home/abhishekrao/repos/wandering-light/.codex/worktrees/review-163-164/docs/metrics.md) already makes wall-clock primary. Forward-pass counts are inadequate: batch sizes and encoded-state sizes differ. Report scored states/examples and inference time as diagnostics. Audit the evaluated revision: this checkout’s [execution counter](/home/abhishekrao/repos/wandering-light/.codex/worktrees/review-163-164/wandering_light/stack_beam.py) counts successful outer example steps, with quotation-body work recorded separately as fuel. That differs from the stated primitive-step counter. Count failed work and specify budget overshoot handling.

4. **The smoke fails the registered acceptance criterion.** It has lower coverage and roughly 45× expenditure. Giving flat the larger budget remains useful diagnostically: does segment search buy anything unavailable through more flat search? It cannot rescue the equal-or-lower-executions claim. Report both readings explicitly; six tasks do not settle the 43-behaviour campaign. Prefer paired solve-versus-budget curves at common, predetermined budgets. Segment-derived per-task budgets answer a conditional question and inherit segment’s allocation across tasks. One cohort-level width answers a different question and need not exhaust the same budget either.

5. **Make verification opportunities identical.** A valid program can end after ordinary top-level glue, without ending at a combinator boundary. Check every complete executable candidate in both searches, at the same point relative to pruning/deduplication, including the empty program if eligible. Fix example and enumeration order: first-hit costs depend on both. Passing shown examples establishes consistency, not generalization; assess first-returned programs on untouched held-out examples without resuming search after failure.

6. **Audit merging and selection.** Equal shown-example states can correspond to programs that disagree elsewhere; representative selection affects held-out success. Remaining depth and any policy history also matter. Raw cumulative scores across different lengths favor shorter paths. Freeze these rules and cell selection before the full cohort; report per-behaviour gains and losses, not just totals.
