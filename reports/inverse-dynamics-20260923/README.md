# Can the encoder name the op between two states? (inverse-dynamics-probe #166)

**INCONCLUSIVE, as registered: the current encoder reaches 0.962 at 1M triples, between the bars of 0.90 and 0.97.**
- **It can compare states, given enough one-step data.** Its held-out set-accuracy climbs 0.841, 0.930, 0.962 across 10k, 100k and 1M triples. It is 0.958 on pairs never seen in training. It fails most on deep stacks: 0.899 with 4-7 items.
- **One explicit comparison feature closes most of the gap.** Position-by-position flags for the top 8 items reach 0.945 at 10k and 0.994 at 1M.
- **Aligned learns fastest but ties at 1M.** Comparing values pairwise from the top reaches 0.992 at 100k and 0.994 at 1M.
- **On the 265 real last-op states from campaign 2,** where the exit-frame oracle policy paid half the world arm's deficit, the three encoders reach 0.966, 0.989 and 0.981 at 1M.

So the campaign-2 last-op miss was mostly the training data, not the architecture. That policy was never trained on one-step targets, and the current encoder learns them. The comparison feature buys data efficiency and depth.

[Registration](https://github.com/abhishekraok/wandering-light/issues/166) posted before any code ran. Driver: `experiments/inverse_dynamics.py`, run dir `checkpoints/inverse-dynamics-20260923`.

## Setup

**Data: triples (s, op, s'), all generated by execution, nothing authored.**
- **s** is a product state over a task's examples, taken from replaying world-arm fit-task witnesses (v4 and world-v3). It is a machine state with no open mark, or a defined frame.
- **op** is every legal op other than `[` and `]`.
- **The label** is the set of ops reaching s'.
- **Held-out** states come from the world arm's holdout tasks: even-numbered ones for validation, odd for test.
- **Truncation:** no state or target in any split is truncated.
- **Recurrence:** exact (s, s') pairs can recur across splits, so accuracy on unseen pairs is reported beside the registered figure.

**Arms,** each trained with a set-likelihood loss, GPU, batch 256, early stopping on validation loss:

| arm | parameters | learning rate (one-epoch probe at 100k) |
|---|---:|---:|
| current (`StackPairPolicy`, s' as its target) | 655,836 | 4e-3, the top of the probed range |
| difference (current plus position flags) | 659,932 | 1e-3 |
| aligned (values compared pairwise from the top) | 968,348 | 1e-3 |

Codex reviewed the driver before the full run. It found one bug, in the strata only, fixed before training.

## What it means

- **The current encoder is not blind to comparison.** It needs a lot of one-step data and struggles past three stack items.
- **An explicit comparison makes it data-efficient and robust at depth.** That is one extra token per compared position.
- **The last-op miss in campaign 2 (#160) was mostly the training distribution.** That policy trained only on the task's final output as its target. A policy trained with near targets, or given a comparison, should settle the last op. The segment-reset search (#168) supplies near targets for free: every closed state is one.
